# Accessibility notes

Plinth is built so that every customer can browse and buy, whether they use a mouse, a keyboard, a touch screen or a screen reader. The theme handles the structure. Some of the result depends on the content you add.

## What the theme does

- **Keyboard access.** Every menu, filter, swatch, tab, slider and button can be reached and used with the keyboard. The focus ring is clearly visible on light, dark and accent backgrounds.
- **Skip link.** The first Tab press on any page offers a link to skip to the main content.
- **Drawers and overlays.** The cart drawer, search overlay, mobile menu and filter drawer hold keyboard focus while open, close with Escape, and return focus to the button that opened them.
- **Reduced motion.** If a customer has asked their device to reduce motion, animations are turned off, the Announcement bar and Ticker stop moving, and videos wait for the customer to press Play.
- **Moving content can be paused.** The Announcement bar and the Ticker each have a Pause button when they autoplay.
- **Colour contrast.** The preset colours meet a contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for lines and icons on every background.
- **Meaning does not rely on colour alone.** Inventory status uses a shape as well as a line of text. Unavailable variants are marked for screen readers as well as visually.
- **Announcements for screen readers.** Changes such as the number of results after filtering, items added to the cart and form errors are announced.
- **Before and after.** The handle is a real slider that works with the arrow keys.
- **Headings.** Each page has one main heading. On the home page, it is the Hero's **Headline**.
- **Right-to-left languages** are supported.

## What you can do

### Images

- Add **alt text** to images when you upload them: say what the image shows in a short sentence. For product images, describe the product and the view ("Blue enamel jug, side view").
- Leave alt text empty for images that are purely decorative.
- Avoid putting important text inside an image. Customers using screen readers or translation cannot read it.

### Text and headings

- Write headings that describe the section. "Our materials" helps more than "Discover".
- Write link and button labels that make sense on their own. "Shop lighting" is better than "Click here".
- Keep Hero headlines and Statement lines short so they stay readable at large sizes and on phones.

### Colour

- If you change scheme colours, check the contrast between text and background, and between accent and background. Aim for at least 4.5:1. See [Colour and grounds](./colour).
- Do not use colour alone to make a point in your own text ("items in red are on sale").

### Video

- For an uploaded video in the **Film** section, add a **Captions file** (a WebVTT .vtt file uploaded under **Content > Files**) if the video has speech.
- For YouTube or Vimeo videos, add captions on that service.
- Add a **Caption** that says what the video shows.

### Annotated products and sets

- Pins on the **Annotated product** and **Shop the set** sections are numbered buttons, and the same information is listed as text beside or under the image. Keep the row labels and values meaningful on their own.

### Apps

Apps add their own content to your store. If an app's widget cannot be used with a keyboard or has low-contrast text, raise it with the app's developer.

## Reporting a problem

If you find an accessibility problem in the theme itself, please [tell us](../contact/). Include the page, the device and browser, any assistive technology you were using, and what happened.
